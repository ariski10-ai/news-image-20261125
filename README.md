# The World in One Day — Wednesday, 25 November 2026

Entry for make.fun's **"Sum up 25 November's world news in one image"**
(news-image-20261125). `main.ts` copies `entry.png` to the output; the image
is the entry.

## What the image shows

A newspaper front page for **Wednesday, 25 November 2026**. Every story was
checked against at least two independent reports published that day
(Reuters, AP, the BBC, Xinhua and wires):

1. **Tehran says tankers hit mines** — Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two
   tankers struck mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz; via Qatari
   mediators Tehran offered to reopen the strait within 48 hours if the US
   lifts its blockade. (An Iranian claim, labelled as such.)
2. **Tanker fire kills 31 in Cairo** — a fuel tanker overturned and ignited
   on the Ring Road in El Warraq, Giza, on Wednesday evening; at least 31
   dead and 21 injured, Egypt's Health Ministry said.
3. **US proposes tariffs on 14 partners** — a Section 301 "excess capacity"
   proposal naming China, Japan, the EU and 11 others, headline rates of
   20–40% capped lower under trade deals; nothing in force before a
   25 January hearing.
4. **Asia markets plunge on AI rout** — closing figures: Kospi −6.2%
   (Samsung −9.9%, SK Hynix −11.1%), Nikkei −2.8% (SoftBank −14.8%), Taiex
   −4.8%, a day after Nvidia slid 5.9% in New York.
5. **All hostages freed in Cartagena** — Colombian forces stormed the Éxito
   supermarket at dawn and freed everyone held since Tuesday's failed
   armoured-car robbery; one gunman was killed.
6. **Pakistan's first astronaut** — Shenzhou-24 docked at Tiangong carrying
   Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, the first foreign astronaut aboard the station and
   the first Pakistani in orbit.

Ticker along the bottom: a flooded illegal gold mine at Pongkor, Bogor
(9 dead, 17 trapped) · Kyiv says five Russian jets were hit in an air-base
strike · Australia and Solomon Islands sign a security treaty in Canberra.

## How it was made

The six panels are AI illustrations made for this entry; every word on the
page — masthead, the red date band naming the day, headlines, kickers and
the ticker — is drawn programmatically over them, so the text stays exact
and legible at small sizes. The layout is a classic front page: the date
cannot be missed, the six most consequential stories get a picture each,
and the ticker carries three more for breadth across regions.
